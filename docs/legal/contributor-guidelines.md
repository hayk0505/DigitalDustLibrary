# Digital Dust Library — Contributor Guidelines

*Draft. Intended to be shown to a new Author the first time they log into
the admin panel (a one-time popup/modal, dismissable with an "I understand"
acknowledgment) and also available as a standing reference page. Not a legal
document — that's the separate Contributor Agreement — this is the practical
"how to publish here" reference.*

## Welcome

You're publishing under the Digital Dust Library name, alongside other
authors. These are the rules of the road — read them once, keep them handy.

## How publishing works here

Every post moves through a simple pipeline:

**Draft → Pending Review → Published** (or **Changes Requested**, sent back
to you with reviewer notes if something needs fixing before it can go out).

As an Author, you can only submit and edit your own posts. An Editor or the
Owner reviews everything before it goes live — either approving and
publishing it, or sending it back with specific feedback. If you get
Changes Requested, revise and resubmit; there's no limit on how many rounds
that takes.

## What to write

Posts fall into one of four categories — **Tech**, **Marginalia**, **Cosmic
Dust**, or **Gaming** — and one of two formats:

- **Minis** — short, a single thought or observation, no minimum length
  requirement beyond "actually finished."
- **Shelf** — full long-form essays, the site's deep-dive format.

Pick whichever fits what you're writing; don't force a short idea into a
long-form shape or vice versa.

## Voice and quality bar

Write like you mean it — well-researched, your own analysis, not
regurgitated summary. If you're stating something as fact, it should hold
up (cite a source in the text or link it if it's not common knowledge).
Editors will send back anything that reads like filler or that makes claims
you can't back up.

## Images — read this carefully

You need the right to publish any image you upload. That means one of:

- **Your own original work** — a photo you took, a graphic you made.
- **Public domain** images.
- **Openly licensed stock photography** from sources like Unsplash, Pexels,
  Pixabay, or Wikimedia Commons — check the specific license on each image,
  since not all Wikimedia Commons images are cleared for this kind of use.
- **Stock you've properly licensed** — a paid license you personally hold
  covers you; a colleague's or a different project's license does not.

**Not okay:** pulling an image from a Google Images search, another
website, or social media without verifying you're actually allowed to use
it. No watermark doesn't mean no copyright — most images online are
protected whether or not they're marked.

If a license requires attribution, include the credit line in the post
(caption or a note near the image).

**AI-generated images are allowed but must be disclosed** — note in the
post that an image was AI-generated and, if relevant, which tool. This is
partly a transparency issue and partly because the copyright status of
AI-generated images is still legally unsettled in a lot of jurisdictions —
better to be upfront.

You don't need to resize or compress anything before uploading — the media
library automatically optimizes images on upload (downscales large images,
converts to a web-friendly format). Just upload the best-quality original
you have.

## Content standards

- No plagiarism — your own words, your own analysis; direct quotes need
  attribution.
- No hate speech, harassment, or doxxing.
- No unverified claims presented as settled fact.
- If a piece was substantially AI-assisted in the writing (not just
  research), disclose that in the post.

## Byline and bio

Your name appears on every post you publish. Once the Authors page is live,
your bio and links will show there too — you'll be able to set that up
yourself.

## Questions

Reach out to an Editor or the Owner if anything here is unclear before you
publish, not after.
